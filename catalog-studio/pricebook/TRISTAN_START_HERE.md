# Tristan: what you sell and what you do

## The product in one sentence

You sell a distributor a permanent copy of a pricebook program: they put in their existing product data, check costs and dealer prices, and produce their own catalogs and price sheets without depending on you to run it.

**Proposed price: $12,500 once per business.** The buyer keeps the delivered version and can maintain its source internally. You retain the right to license the same underlying product to other businesses. This is a proposed nonexclusive license, not an exclusive sale of all intellectual property.

The price is not validated. A basic PDF catalog generator alone would be a weak $10,000 offer. The value here must come from a buyer's repeated pricing work, multiple dealer books, costly review effort and need for a locally operated program.

## The realistic meaning of “sell it and be done”

No seller-operated server, recurring processing, API bill or mandatory subscription is built into this product. Delivery ends after a defined acceptance process and a fixed correction window. Future improvements, browser compatibility work, new file formats, extra layouts and maintenance are separate work for the buyer or a separately engaged developer.

This does not mean no work per sale. You still qualify the business, demonstrate the program, test their approved sample, confirm the written scope, collect payment, deliver and resolve in-scope acceptance defects. There is no scheduled staff training in the offer; the buyer follows the included guide. A buyer that needs live training or managed publishing is outside this offer.

## Who could justify the price

Focus first on independent electrical or industrial distributors that:

1. Already maintain a usable export with SKU, name, category, selling unit, list price and cost.
2. Have up to 1,000 active SKUs per complete pricebook and two to five simple global price tiers.
3. Rebuild or check dealer pricebooks repeatedly, with substantial measured effort.
4. Can approve their own source data and published output.
5. Can operate local files and backups using their existing computers.

Qualify the workflow before pitching the price. A company with a small, rarely updated catalog or a good existing system may have no reason to buy this. A company needing live ERP synchronization, 20,000 SKUs, complex contract prices or multiuser collaboration needs a different product.

## Questions to ask on the call

- How often do prices or catalogs change?
- Who rebuilds the dealer books, and how many hours does one full cycle take?
- How many products and dealer tiers are involved? Are discounts global percentages or individual contract prices?
- Does cost mean cost per the same selling unit as the list price?
- Can you export those columns today without giving me website access?
- Who verifies the supplier data and authorizes the final published book?
- Would a standalone program with files on your computer fit your process?

## A value calculation to use honestly

Use the buyer's measured time and agreed hourly value:

`(old hours per cycle − new hours per cycle) × cycles per year × hourly value`

Illustration only: 40 hours before, six hours afterward, 12 cycles/year, $50/hour gives $20,400/year of released capacity. At that assumed value, $12,500 represents roughly 7.4 months of benefit. Released staff capacity is not automatically reduced payroll or extra revenue.

An internal qualification rule: require at least $37,500 of buyer-supported capacity value over 24 months before pursuing a $12,500 sale. That is your screening threshold, not a guarantee or market fact. Do not add hypothetical prevented losses to make a weak case look strong.
